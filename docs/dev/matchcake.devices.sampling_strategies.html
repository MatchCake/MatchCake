class matchcake.devices.sampling_strategies.k_qubits_by_k_qubits_sampling.KQubitsByKQubitsSampling¶

Bases: SamplingStrategy

Autoregressive sampler that draws K qubits per step.

Samples are generated block by block following the chain rule of probability:

  1. Sample \(x_0\) from the marginal \(\pi_0(x_0)\).

  2. For \(j = 1\) to \(n - 1\), sample \(x_j\) from the conditional \(\pi_j(x_0, \dots, x_{j-1}, x_j) / \pi_{j-1}(x_0, \dots, x_{j-1})\).

  3. Return \(x_0 \dots x_{n-1}\).

The implementation is backend agnostic. The probability callback may return NumPy arrays, Torch tensors, or any other qml.math backend; all probability arithmetic stays in that backend and only the discrete binary samples are materialised as NumPy integers. Arbitrary leading batch dimensions on the probability outputs are supported.

matchcake.devices.sampling_strategies.product_basis_sampling module¶

class matchcake.devices.sampling_strategies.product_basis_sampling.ProductBasisSampling(shots_chunk_size: int | None = None, memory_budget: int | None = None, device: str | device | None = None, dtype: dtype | None = None, deduplicate: bool = True)¶

Bases: SamplingStrategy

Sampler for measurements in an arbitrary single-qubit product basis.

Draws samples of a matchgate circuit applied to a product state, with each qubit \(k\) measured along its own Bloch direction \(\hat{n}_k\), i.e. measuring the observable \(\hat{n}_k \cdot \vec{\sigma}_k\). Outcome bit 0 stands for the eigenvalue \(+1\) and bit 1 for \(-1\), so (0, 0, 1) is the computational basis and (1, 0, 0) the X basis. Such measurements leave the matchgate set, since rotating a qubit to the X basis takes a Hadamard, but they can still be sampled efficiently [Brod2016] (Theorem 3 and Sec. III.1).

The sampler follows Brod’s construction. An ancilla in \(|+\rangle\) is placed before the first qubit, with Majorana operators \(a_0 = X_A\) and \(a_1 = Y_A\). On the states it meets the ancilla is always in \(|+\rangle\), so \(X_A = 1\). Its Majorana \(a_1\) is the parity index of the extended covariance matrix \(\tilde\Lambda\) (see extended_covariance_matrix()), which therefore describes the product state with the ancilla as a Gaussian state. The circuit acts on it through the lifted single-particle transition matrix.

The qubits are then measured one at a time, from the first to the last. When qubit \(k\) is reached, the qubits \(l < k\) are in computational basis states with known values of \(Z_l\), and on the current state

\[X_k = -i\,\sigma_k\, a_1 c_{2k}, \qquad Y_k = -i\,\sigma_k\, a_1 c_{2k+1}, \qquad Z_k = -i\, c_{2k} c_{2k+1},\]

where \(\sigma_k = \prod_{l<k} Z_l = \pm 1\). All three are quadratic in the Majorana operators \((a_1, c_{2k}, c_{2k+1})\), and a single-qubit rotation of qubit \(k\) acts as a rotation in \(SO(3)\) of these three operators, which keeps the state Gaussian. Each step therefore:

  1. Rotates the triple \((a_1, c_{2k}, c_{2k+1})\) so that \(\hat{n}_k \cdot \vec{\sigma}_k\) becomes \(-i\, c_{2k} c_{2k+1}\). The new \(a_1\) is the direction \(w = (n_z, -\sigma_k n_y, \sigma_k n_x)\) of the old triple.

  2. Measures \(Z_k\) on the rotated state. The outcome \(\lambda = \pm 1\) has probability \((1 - \lambda \tilde\Lambda_{2k, 2k+1}) / 2\).

  3. Projects onto the outcome with the rank-2 update

    \[\tilde\Lambda_{ij} \leftarrow \tilde\Lambda_{ij} + \lambda \frac{\tilde\Lambda_{2k, i} \tilde\Lambda_{2k+1, j} - \tilde\Lambda_{2k, j} \tilde\Lambda_{2k+1, i}}{1 - \lambda \tilde\Lambda_{2k, 2k+1}},\]

    and updates \(\sigma_{k+1} = \lambda\, \sigma_k\).

After the rotation qubit \(k\) is left in a computational basis state, which is what makes \(\sigma_{k+1}\) a number for the next step. The rotation and the update only change the probabilities of the qubits already measured, not those of the others, so the outcomes follow the distribution of the original measurement. When all the bases are the computational basis, the rotations are the identity and the sampler draws ordinary computational basis samples.

The probability of a complete outcome is the product of the conditional probabilities and is computed exactly by outcome_probabilities(). Marginal probabilities on a subset of the qubits are not provided, since whether they can be computed efficiently is open [Brod2016] (Sec. IV).

Cost and implementation. Step \(k\) touches a matrix of size \(d = 2(n - k) + 1\): \(O(d)\) for the rotation and \(O(d^2)\) for the rank-2 update, so a shot costs \(\sum_k d^2 \approx \tfrac{4}{3} n^3 = O(n^3)\), with no Pfaffian. The work is organized to be efficient on both CPU and GPU:

  • The ancilla is kept as the last index, so the matrix of the unmeasured qubits is always a trailing block of a single buffer, updated in place. The update uses baddbmm_ on CPU and two addcmul_ on CUDA, the fastest kernels on each.

  • The two rotations of each qubit (one per sign \(\sigma_k\)) are computed once, and the random numbers are drawn on the compute device, so the loop over the qubits runs without host synchronization once every shot has its own matrix.

  • Shots with the same outcomes so far share a single matrix, together with the number of shots that follow it (prefix deduplication). The split of these shots between the two outcomes of the next qubit is drawn from a binomial distribution, which is the same as drawing each shot independently. This saves most of the work for states with few likely outcomes. Once the number of distinct prefixes exceeds DEDUPLICATION_THRESHOLD times the number of shots, every shot gets its own matrix.

  • The shots are processed in chunks that fit a memory budget, so the matrices of a chunk stay within memory. See shots_per_chunk().

Parameters:

  • shots_chunk_size (Optional[int]) – Number of shots processed together. Defaults to None, in which case it is computed from memory_budget by shots_per_chunk().

  • memory_budget (Optional[int]) – Memory, in bytes, that the matrices of a chunk may use. Defaults to None: DEFAULT_CPU_MEMORY_BUDGET on CPU, and DEFAULT_CUDA_MEMORY_FRACTION of the free memory on CUDA.

  • device (Optional[Union[str, torch.device]]) – Torch device the samples are computed on, e.g. "cuda". Defaults to None, the device of the extended covariance matrix.

  • dtype (Optional[torch.dtype]) – Floating point dtype of the computation. Defaults to None, the dtype of the extended covariance matrix when it is float32 or float64, and float64 otherwise. float32 is faster, in particular on consumer GPUs, at the cost of precision on large circuits.

  • deduplicate (bool) – Whether shots with the same outcomes so far share their matrix. Defaults to True.

static measurement_frames(bloch_vectors: Tensor, signs: Tensor) → Tensor¶

Build the rotations that turn the measured observable into \(Z_k\).

The rows of each rotation are the new \((a_1, c_{2k}, c_{2k+1})\) written in the old ones: \(w = (n_z, -\sigma n_y, \sigma n_x)\), then two unit vectors \(u, v\) orthogonal to \(w\) with \(u \times v = w\). The rotation is proper, and \(-i\,(u \cdot c)(v \cdot c) = \hat{n} \cdot \vec{\sigma}\).

Parameters:
